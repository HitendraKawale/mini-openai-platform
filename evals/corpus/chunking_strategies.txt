Chunking is the process of splitting a document into smaller pieces before embedding them.
If chunks are too large, the embedding averages too many ideas and retrieval precision drops.
If chunks are too small, individual chunks lose the surrounding context needed to answer questions.
A common baseline is fixed-size chunking with overlap, where consecutive chunks share a band of words so that sentences cut at a boundary still appear intact in one chunk.
Sentence-aware chunking splits on sentence boundaries instead of word counts, which keeps every chunk grammatically complete.
The overlap region also protects against losing facts that straddle two chunks.
Retrieval quality is usually evaluated with recall at k and mean reciprocal rank against a golden set of questions.
